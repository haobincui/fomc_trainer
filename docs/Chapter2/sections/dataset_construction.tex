\section{Dataset Construction}\label{ch2:sec:the-dataset}

This section describes the two source layers used in the completed experiment.
Official Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) Minutes supply a training-only
style abstraction, whereas point-in-time macroeconomic observations supply the
factual model input.  The two layers are linked through a controlled topic
taxonomy, but the contemporaneous Minutes text is not paired with an indicator
card as a supervised answer target.


\subsection*{FOMC Minutes and Indicators}\label{ch2:sec:raw_data}


\paragraph*{Document corpus and meeting roster.}
The implemented roster contains 128 FOMC meetings from January 28, 2009
through January 29, 2025.  Meetings, rather than individual paragraphs, are the
unit used for temporal partitioning: 102 meetings are assigned to training, 13
to validation, and 13 to the held-out test partition.  All observations
associated with a meeting remain in the same partition.

Post-2009 Minutes are used only to construct the institutional style guide for
the analysis task.  The style source contains 3,890 extracted training rows
from the 102 training meetings and produces 58 section-style entries.  The
abstraction retains discourse properties such as neutral narration, evidence
ordering, restrained contrast, and uncertainty calibration.  It does not pass
the underlying excerpt, numerical values, dates, named individuals,
meeting-specific events, or policy outcomes to the model.  Validation- and
test-period Minutes are not used to estimate this guide.  Consequently, the
same-meeting Minutes never serve as factual input or as the supervised response
target for \textit{Model chk-1}.

Minutes from 1993 through 2008 are retained as a supplementary archive because
their formatting and section structure differ from the post-2009 corpus.  They
are not pooled into the completed \textit{Model chk-0}--\textit{Model chk-1}
training corpus or the main \textit{Model chk-1}--\textit{Model chk-3}
comparison reported in this chapter.

\paragraph*{Point-in-time indicator evidence.}
The factual source for the completed analysis experiment is a set of sealed
ALFRED vintage snapshots organized under the 26-topic controlled vocabulary.
The initial roster contains 2,287 unique meeting--atomic-topic pairs.  Applying
the \(D-1\) availability rule removes 170 pairs for which no admissible vintage
can be established, leaving 2,117 prepared fact cards.  These cards contain
16,722 evidence items in total; an individual card contains between 3 and 18
items, with a mean of 7.90.  Every item is bound to an evidence identifier,
series identifier, observation date, unit, value, availability basis, and
source hash.  Later revisions and meeting-day observations are excluded.

Table~\ref{tab:ch2:summary_statis} provides a hierarchical overview of the

the remaining prose into controlled style atoms.  The 58 entries therefore
constitute a source catalogue.  The completed experiment selects only two
entries from that catalogue: the economic-situation style, estimated from 943
rows, and the financial-situation style, estimated from 1,060 rows.  Token
budgets are evaluated separately on the rendered model inputs; no prepared
fact-card prompt is truncated, and the longest final prompt--response sequence
contains 4,019 tokens.




\subsection*{From FOMC Minutes to Analysis}


Because our prompts are constructed from macroeconomic and financial indicators, we must map unstructured Minutes paragraphs to the corresponding indicator topics. We use an LLM (ChatGPT-4o) as a \textit{weakly supervised classifier} to assign indicator labels to Minutes paragraphs, which enables systematic alignment between numerical inputs and textual targets. Since LLM-based labeling can introduce noise or bias, we mitigate this risk by (i) manually cleaning and standardizing the label ontology and (ii) relabeling with a constrained reference list after cleaning. Importantly, we exclude administrative paragraphs and the ``Committee Policy Action'' content from the \textit{analysis-generation} dataset to avoid leaking realized policy decisions into training, which is crucial for a fair decision-prediction evaluation later in the chapter.


To construct candidate indicator labels, we first asked the LLM to label paragraphs without a predefined reference list. These model-generated tags are treated as \textit{raw labels}. We then identified several quality issues that required cleaning and standardization.

First, some of the raw labels were too general or lacked measurable indicators—for example, labels like ``Housing'', ``International Markets'', ``Swap Arrangements'', and ``Liquidity Facilities''. These categories do not correspond to specific economic indicators.

Second, different labels were often used for the same underlying indicator. For instance, ``PCE Price Index'', ``Consumer Spending'', and ``Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE)'' all refer to the same economic concept and were consolidated under ``Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE)''.


Finally, some labels represented subcomponents of broader constructs. For example, ``Total Assets of the Federal Reserve'' and ``Total Liabilities of the Federal Reserve'' were consolidated under ``Federal Reserve Balance Sheet''.


Therefore, we manually cleaned the \textit{raw labels} and created a list of \textit{reference labels} based on them. We then asked the LLM to relabel the paragraphs using this refined set of reference labels.

Our raw corpus contains 18,957 paragraphs. We removed paragraphs related to ``Secretary'', ``Attendance'', and ``Voting for this action'' because they contain no substantive analytical content. We also removed ``Committee Policy Action'' paragraphs from the analysis-generation dataset, because these paragraphs primarily summarize realized decisions rather than the underlying discussion; excluding them also reduces the risk of decision-information leakage when we later evaluate the model’s ability to infer policy actions from analytical context. After filtering, 6,266 paragraphs remained for indicator labeling.



Next, we introduced two fallback tags: ``Non-Core'' for paragraphs without analytical content, and ``Other'' when no relevant indicator could be mapped. Because some paragraphs refer to multiple indicators, the model was allowed to assign multiple labels separated by commas. This stage produced 5,290 labeled analytical paragraphs, which expanded to 5,397 rows across 37 raw labels.


We then manually standardized the raw labels into 26 \textit{reference-label} categories and relabeled all 6,266 filtered paragraphs using this controlled taxonomy. ``Other'' was assigned when no indicator matched, and ``Non-Core'' when the paragraph contained no analytical content.




Following controlled relabeling and prompt construction, 4,888 candidate
question--answer records were aligned by their source-row indices.  Three of
these records had empty archived responses.  Two responses were recovered from
the existing raw-data backfill, whereas the remaining record had no admissible
backfill and was therefore excluded.  The resulting canonical main corpus
contains 4,887 records from 128 meetings between January 28, 2009 and January
29, 2025.

The value 3,890 denotes the training partition of this canonical corpus rather
than the number of raw paragraphs or standardized-label occurrences.  To avoid
cross-meeting leakage, the 128 meeting dates were ordered chronologically and
partitioned at the meeting level.  The first 102 meetings, spanning January 28,
2009 through November 3, 2021, contribute 3,890 records to training; the next
13 meetings contribute 535 validation records; and the final 13 meetings
contribute 462 test records.  Thus,
\(3{,}890+535+462=4{,}887\).  The row counts are not proportional to the number
of meetings because the number of retained meeting--section--topic records
varies across meetings.  Table~\ref{tab:ch2:indicator_count} instead summarizes
upstream reference-label occurrences and should not be interpreted as the

\subsection*{Economic and Financial Market Indicators}

Guided by the reference-label taxonomy, we collected macroeconomic series from the FRED database and financial-market series from WRDS. FRED offers country-level macroeconomic data, while WRDS provides standardized access to widely used asset-pricing variables and benchmark series.

For macroeconomic coverage, we include GDP growth, Consumer Price Index measures, Federal Funds Rate series, unemployment measures, trade indicators, and Treasury yields. For financial coverage, we include equity indices, commodity prices, volatility indices, Federal Reserve balance-sheet measures, overnight-rate series, and corporate-bond yields.

\subsection*{Construction of Distilled Reasoning Targets}

The analysis task requires more than reproducing an observed paragraph: the
model must identify recent movements in a point-in-time fact card, compare
observations across dates, distinguish direct observations from arithmetic
implications, and calibrate uncertainty without introducing unsupported causal
claims.  The supervised completion therefore contains an intermediate
reasoning component in addition to a concise final analysis.  This is an
instance of response distillation (\cite{hinton2015distilling}), in which a
stronger teacher produces task-specific targets for the smaller
\textit{Model chk-0} initialization.

The implemented teacher is \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro}, queried with thinking
enabled and high reasoning effort.  Its input is restricted to an atomic topic,
the corresponding \(D-1\) fact card, a style-only guide abstracted from the
training partition, and a machine-readable output contract.  The model-visible
fact card contains the series identifier, observation date, unit, value,
evidence identifier, availability basis, and source hash for each retained
observation.  Identity and cutoff fields---including the meeting date, sample
identifier, and availability timestamp---remain in the audit lineage but are
removed from the model-visible projection.  The style guide describes discourse
features but supplies no meeting-specific facts.  In particular, the teacher
receives no same-meeting Minutes excerpt,
realized rate change, decision label, archived answer, or other target text in
the training, validation, or test partition.  This restriction is identical
across partitions and is enforced before teacher generation rather than only at
evaluation time.

Table~\ref{tab:ch2:prompt_for_data_distillation} summarizes the frozen teacher
contract.  The teacher's provider-side \texttt{reasoning\_content} is retained
as the initial reasoning trace \(r_i\).  The visible response must be one JSON
object with exactly two fields: a plain-text \texttt{answer} and a non-empty,
duplicate-free list of \texttt{evidence\_ids}.  The evidence identifiers are
used to validate provenance and grounding but are not printed in the final
analysis.  The supervised completion is materialized as
\(r_i\Vert\texttt{</think>}\Vert a_i\), where \(a_i\) is the value of the
teacher's \texttt{answer} field.  The student prompt omits the teacher-only
output schema and retains only the analysis instruction, atomic topic, fact
card, and style guide.

\paragraph*{Derivation of the final SFT split.}
The sample unit is a meeting--atomic-topic pair rather than a meeting.  The 102
meetings in the training period therefore produce 1,885 distinct canonical
topic observations.  Of these, 170 are removed because no admissible \(D-1\)
vintage can be established, leaving 1,715 teacher-generation candidates.  Two
teacher results fail the structured-output contract and 30 have an empty
required response component.  The canonical training set is consequently
\begin{equation}
    N_{\mathrm{train}}^{\mathrm{canonical}}
    =1{,}885-170-2-30=1{,}683.
    \label{eq:ch2:chk1_train_canonical_count}
\end{equation}
The corresponding validation and test populations begin with 205 and 197
meeting--topic observations.  Six validation observations and seven test
observations have an empty required response component; hence their admitted
counts are \(205-6=199\) and \(197-7=190\), respectively.  Across the three
partitions, teacher generation is thus attempted for 2,117 observations and
canonical admission retains 2,072.

Only the training partition is subject to the stage-specific role allocation.
For each of its 1,683 admitted observations, the role is determined by
\begin{equation}
    h_i=\operatorname{int}_{16}
    \!\left(\operatorname{SHA256}(\texttt{sample\_id}_i)\right)\bmod 100.
    \label{eq:ch2:chk1_sft_role_hash}
\end{equation}
Values 0--69 assign 1,190 observations to the SFT-only subset, values 90--99
assign 165 observations to a shared subset that is also eligible for SFT, and
values 70--89 reserve 328 observations for an earlier reward-training design.
The initial SFT training population is therefore
\begin{equation}
    N_{\mathrm{train}}^{\mathrm{SFT}}
    =1{,}190+165=1{,}355,
    \label{eq:ch2:chk1_train_sft_count}
\end{equation}
whereas all 199 validation and 190 test observations remain available for SFT
evaluation.  This produces 1,744 SFT-eligible observations in total.

Their teacher traces are compressed with \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} at maximum
reasoning effort while the corresponding final answers are held fixed.  The
compression target is 512--2,400 tokens; the realized traces range from 517 to
1,600 tokens, with a median of 996 tokens.  A subsequent source-only audit
removes one training observation with a material unit-conversion error, giving
\(1{,}355-1=1{,}354\) final training observations.  The final \textit{Model
chk-1} SFT corpus therefore contains 1,743 observations: 1,354 for training,
199 for validation, and 190 for testing.  The later deterministic repair and
source-grounding qualification are reported with the complete sample flow in
Table~\ref{tab:ch2:chk0_chk1_sample_flow}.




\subsection*{Synthetic Text Generation}

downstream alignment stage that trains the model to rewrite analysis into
FOMC-Minutes style.

passed directly to the Minutes-rewriting stage; no official Minutes excerpt is
added to the prompt.  This construction isolates the effect of task-specific

\textit{chk-1} baseline and \textit{chk-3}.

\subsection*{Construction of the \textit{Model chk-0}--\textit{Model chk-1}
Analysis Dataset}

\paragraph*{Task definition.}
The transition from \textit{Model chk-0} to \textit{Model chk-1} is a
supervised domain-adaptation stage.  \textit{Model chk-0} denotes the unadapted
\texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B} initialization, whereas \textit{Model
chk-1} denotes the model obtained after supervised fine-tuning on
point-in-time economic-analysis examples.  Thus, \textit{Model chk-0} is the
initialization for this transition; it is not the baseline in the subsequent
\textit{Model chk-1}--\textit{Model chk-3} comparison.  The learning objective
is to map the information available before an FOMC meeting to a concise,
evidence-grounded analysis of one economic or financial topic.

\paragraph*{Canonical unit and chronological partition.}
The unit of observation is a meeting--atomic-topic pair,
\(i=(m,k)\), where \(m\) indexes an FOMC meeting and \(k\) belongs to the
26-topic controlled vocabulary reported above.  Multiple legacy paragraphs
that refer to the same pair are collapsed into a single canonical key
\((D_m,k)\), where \(D_m\) is the meeting date.  Administrative labels such as
\textit{Other}, \textit{Non-Core}, and \textit{Delete} are excluded before the
canonical population is formed.  This procedure yields 2,287 unique
meeting--topic observations covering 128 meetings.

The partition is chronological and is fixed before target generation.  The
training partition contains 102 meetings from January 28, 2009 through
November 3, 2021; the validation partition contains 13 meetings from December
15, 2021 through June 14, 2023; and the held-out test partition contains 13
meetings from July 26, 2023 through January 29, 2025.  No meeting appears in
more than one partition.  This meeting-level separation is stricter than a
random row split because all topics associated with the same policy meeting
remain in the same partition.

\paragraph*{Point-in-time evidence construction.}
For a meeting on date \(D_m\), the admissible information set is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I}_{m,k}^{D-1}
    =\left\{e:\; A(e)\leq D_m-1,\; K(e)=k\right\},
    \label{eq:ch2:chk1_d1_information_set}
\end{equation}
where \(A(e)\) is the verified availability date of evidence item \(e\) and
\(K(e)\) is its atomic topic.  Macroeconomic vintages are retrieved from sealed
ALFRED snapshots, and every retained item is bound to its series identifier,
observation date, unit, value, evidence identifier, source hash, and verified
availability upper bound.  Meeting-day information, revisions released after
the cutoff, the Minutes for meeting \(m\), the realized policy action, and the
associated decision label are not included.  If no source for a meeting--topic
pair has a provable \(D-1\) vintage, that pair is excluded rather than filled
using a later vintage or an imputed value.

For each series, the fact-card builder first attempts to retain the latest
three admissible observations.  If the resulting prompt exceeds the 4,096-token
preparation budget, the number of recent observations is reduced from three to
two and then to one; exceeding the budget after this adaptive selection is
treated as an error rather than resolved by silent truncation.  In the realized
corpus, all 2,117 prepared prompts satisfy the budget without truncation.  The
fact cards contain between 3 and 18 evidence items, with a mean of 7.90.  Of
the 2,287 canonical pairs, 170 training-period pairs are excluded because an
admissible \(D-1\) vintage cannot be established, leaving 2,117 prepared
examples.

\paragraph*{Prompt and supervised target.}
Each model input consists of an instruction, a point-in-time fact card, and a
style-only guide.  The guide is abstracted from training-partition FOMC Minutes
and describes features such as neutral third-person narration, evidence-first
organization, restrained contrast, and calibrated uncertainty.  Numerical
values, dates, named individuals, meeting-specific events, and policy outcomes
are removed from this guide.  Consequently, the Minutes contribute only a
controlled stylistic prior; neither the contemporaneous Minutes text nor the
realized decision is supplied as factual input or as the response target for
this stage.

Let \(p_i\) denote the instruction and style guide and let \(d_i\) denote the
serialized fact card.  The supervised example has the form
\begin{equation}
    x_i=(p_i,d_i), \qquad
    y_i=r_i\;\Vert\;\texttt{</think>}\;\Vert\;a_i,
    \label{eq:ch2:chk1_sft_example}
\end{equation}
where \(r_i\) is a teacher-generated reasoning trace and \(a_i\) is the final
analysis.  Targets are generated with \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro} under a
high-reasoning contract and a JSON response format.  The reasoning is
read from the provider's reasoning field, the final analysis is read from the
answer field, and the two components are joined using the model's native
closing reasoning boundary.  The prompt prohibits external events, people,
causal claims, policy actions, and post-cutoff facts, and requires all numbers
and directional statements to be supported by the fact card.

Two of the 2,117 teacher requests return invalid structured results.  Among the
remaining 2,115 results, 43 are rejected because either the reasoning trace or
the final analysis is empty.  Canonical admission therefore retains 2,072
examples: 1,683 for training, 199 for validation, and 190 for testing.  The
admission audit verifies uniqueness of sample identifiers, prompts, responses,
and canonical keys; exact population coverage; disjoint meeting partitions;
the expected topic roster; zero prompt truncation; and zero teacher-prompt
leakage.

\paragraph*{Allocation, reasoning compression, and deterministic repair.}
All validation and test observations are retained for SFT evaluation.  Within
the 1,683 canonical training observations, the training role is assigned by
\(h_i=\operatorname{int}_{16}(\operatorname{SHA256}(\texttt{sample\_id}_i))
\bmod 100\).  Values 0--69 define an SFT-only subset (1,190 observations),
values 90--99 define a shared subset that is also admissible for SFT (165
observations), and values 70--89 identify 328 observations reserved for an
earlier reward-training design.  The latter observations are not used to train
\textit{Model chk-1}.  The initial SFT training population is therefore
\(1{,}190+165=1{,}355\) observations.

The original teacher traces are longer than required for the available
training budget.  They are consequently compressed with
\texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} at maximum reasoning effort, subject to a target
range of 512--2,400 tokens, while the final answer is held fixed.  Compression
completes for all 1,744 SFT-eligible observations.  A source-only semantic audit
then excludes one training observation that converts 4,279.31192 million into
4,279 billion and adds unsupported causal interpretation.  This produces the
final split of 1,354 training, 199 validation, and 190 test observations.

A subsequent deterministic cleaning pass operates on this fixed set of 1,743
observations.  It removes inline evidence identifiers from 159 answers and
recovers 42 malformed answer strings from sealed teacher artifacts.  It also
repairs 42 reasoning traces containing metadata or a repeated answer tail.
Because the answer and reasoning repairs overlap, 237 unique observations are
modified.  Sample order, canonical identifiers, prompts, fact cards, and their
cryptographic hashes remain unchanged.  The final token audit records a maximum
of 4,019 tokens for the complete prompt--response sequence, zero truncation at
4,608 tokens, zero cross-partition duplicates, and valid completion masks for
all 1,743 observations.

\paragraph*{Quality qualification.}
The canonical release passes the encoding, point-in-time, reference-leakage,
schema, split-integrity, target-consistency, teacher-grounding, and token-budget
audits.  These checks establish the integrity of the data lineage and the
absence of mechanical leakage; they do not, however, establish that every
natural-language inference in the teacher target follows from the supplied
evidence.  A two-stage source-only review of the 237 deterministically modified
observations classifies 124 as passing and 113 as containing at least one
factual or causal grounding issue.  The resulting clean-v2 artifact therefore
remains a training candidate rather than a semantically passed release.
\textit{Model chk-1} was trained under an explicit exception scoped to this SFT
stage.  Accordingly, the completed training run demonstrates successful
optimization on the constructed corpus, but it does not warrant the stronger
claim that every supervised target is fully source-grounded.  This limitation
is retained as a threat to validity in interpreting the experimental results.

\subsection*{Dataset for the Completed Minutes-Rewriting Experiment}

The Minutes-rewriting dataset contains 1,683 training observations, 199
validation observations, and 190 test observations.  Each input contains one
\texttt{chk-1} analysis.  The response target contains a synthetic,
teacher-generated fidelity rationale followed by a paragraph written in the
style of the FOMC Minutes and derived from the same analysis; it does not
contain an excerpt from the official Minutes.  The task requires the model to
preserve the substantive claims, numerical quantities, and dates contained in
the analysis while expressing them as a single formal paragraph.  This dataset
therefore defines the implemented \texttt{chk-1}$\rightarrow$\texttt{chk-3}
experiment and the associated baseline comparison.
